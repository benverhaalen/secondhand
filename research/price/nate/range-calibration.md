## 1. Split conformal and conformalized quantile regression (CQR)

- *observed* (arXiv 1905.03222, Romano, Patterson, Candes, submitted 2019-05-08, https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03222): CQR "combines conformal prediction with classical quantile regression". The abstract claims a "theoretical guarantee of valid coverage" and shorter intervals than other conformal methods because it adapts to heteroscedasticity.
- *observed* (same paper, PDF text): in the 11-dataset benchmark of 20 random splits at alpha = 0.1, "all methods based on conformal prediction successfully construct prediction bands at the nominal coverage rate of 90%". The non-conformalized quantile neural network "tends to undercover", and the paper cites other authors seeing quantile-network coverage "ranging from the 95% nominal level ... to well below 50%". The synthetic example in the paper shows CQR 91.06% coverage at average length 1.99 against split conformal 91.4% at 2.91.
- *observed* (same paper): the guarantee is marginal and rests on exchangeable data, and the calibration set must be held out from the one used to fit the quantile models (the "split").
- *inferred*: for a range built from sold comps, the stated low and high are the two quantile heads (for example 10th and 90th percentile of price given item features), and conformal adds one number, a correction measured on a held-out calibration set, that widens or narrows both ends so that coverage is met.
- Published used-car application, *asserted* (search snippet, not opened; the PDF host refused my fetch): a 2023 ISEG Lisbon master's dissertation "Conformal prediction of used car prices" (http://hdl.handle.net/10400.5/31162, PDF https://repositorio.ulisboa.pt/jspui/bitstream/10400.5/31162/1/DM-EB-2023.pdf) applies CQR to a LightGBM model on used-car listings from May 2021 and reports that conformal methods guarantee nominal coverage "at the expense of wider prediction intervals", and that median CQR width grows more than proportionally with nominal coverage compared with ordinary quantile regression. I did not obtain its coverage or MAPE numbers.

## 2. Group-conditional (Mondrian) conformal for per-category coverage

- *observed* (MAPIE docs source, doc/content/conformal-prediction/conditional-guarantees.md, current master, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scikit-learn-contrib/MAPIE/master/doc/content/conformal-prediction/conditional-guarantees.md): "Standard Conformal Prediction provides marginal guarantees ... Some algorithms in MAPIE provide intervals that adapt to specific data points, such as Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR) or the Residual Normalized Score. However, their theoretical guarantees are still marginal." Mondrian conformal "builds prediction sets with a group-conditional coverage guarantee" by stratifying the data by group and applying split conformal independently within each group, with the quantile per group computed from the n^g scores of that group.
- *observed* (same page and MAPIE v1 release notes, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scikit-learn-contrib/MAPIE/master/doc/content/getting-started/v1-release-notes.md): "MAPIE v1 does not provide a dedicated Mondrian implementation"; you split by group and run conformal in each. The page also describes a second, more general "Conditional Conformal Prediction" method with guarantees for all subgroups of interest.
- *observed* (crepes README, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/henrikbostrom/crepes/master/README.md, package 0.9.1): crepes implements "standard, normalized and Mondrian conformal regressors" and conformal predictive systems. A Mondrian regressor is formed by passing a function or a `MondrianCategorizer` as argument `mc` to `calibrate`, and categories can come from binning a difficulty estimate (the README example uses 20 bins). It also has `crepes.martingales` to test the exchangeability assumption with conformal test martingales (README lines 503 to 511).
- *observed* (arXiv 1209.2673, Vovk, "Conditional validity of inductive conformal predictors", 2012-09-12, https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.2673): inductive conformal predictors "have been only known to control unconditional coverage probability"; the paper studies ways to reach conditional validity. This is the original source for the Mondrian construction named in the MAPIE page.
- *inferred*: Mondrian by category gives per-category coverage only if each category has enough calibration sales. The conformal quantile needs on the order of 1/alpha calibration points to be finite at all (at 80% coverage, at least 4 per group for the rank to exist; many more for stable widths). This links to the abstain rule in [what-market-value-is.md](../ben/what-market-value-is.md) line 31 and to the thin-category problem in section 6, where a vendor suppresses categories under 8 sales.
- *inferred*: a hierarchy of groups (category, then brand tier, then price band) with a fallback to the parent group when the child has too few calibration sales is a design choice, not something I found published for resale.

## 3. Conformal under time drift

- *observed* (arXiv 2106.00170, Gibbs and Candes, "Adaptive Conformal Inference Under Distribution Shift", 2021-06-01, https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00170, PDF text): the update is `alpha_{t+1} = alpha_t + gamma (alpha - err_t)` where `err_t` is 1 if the true value fell outside the interval just issued and 0 otherwise. The paper states it "provably achieves the desired coverage frequency over long-time intervals irrespective of the true data generating process", and uses gamma = 0.005 in its experiments, noting larger gamma protects better against shift and a too small gamma behaves like the non-adaptive method. Its two real datasets are stock-market volatility and Fannie Mae-style data series (the PDF text names market volatility explicitly).
- *observed* (same paper): the guarantee is long-run average coverage, not coverage at every step; it works wrapped around any model that gives point predictions or quantiles, which includes CQR.
- *observed* (arXiv 2202.13415, Barber, Candes, Ramdas, Tibshirani, "Conformal prediction beyond exchangeability", https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415): when "the data distribution drifts over time, then the data points are no longer exchangeable"; the paper uses "weighted quantiles to introduce robustness against distribution drift", so recent calibration residuals count more.
- *observed* (MAPIE README on master, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scikit-learn-contrib/MAPIE/master/README.md): "MAPIE in 2026 ... new adaptive conformal prediction methods have been added" and "exchangeability tests have been introduced". MAPIE's `TimeSeriesRegressor` replaces `MapieTimeSeriesRegressor` in v1 (release notes line 25 and 140) and its reference list includes Xu and Xie, "Conformal prediction interval for dynamic time-series" (ICML 2021). I did not open the class API page for the ACI parameter name.
- *inferred*: resale prices drift with season, hype and fee changes, so the calibration residuals should be time-ordered, newest weighted most, and a running miscoverage tracker per category (ACI) is the published way to correct a range that has drifted off its stated share. The per-category tracker needs sales arriving at a rate that makes a window meaningful.

## 5. Days to sell: survival and time-to-event

- *observed* (Opendoor, "Liquidity modeling in real estate using survival analysis", https://www.opendoor.com/articles/liquidity-modeling-real-estate-survival-analysis, undated page): dropping unsold homes biases the model: "The deviation between the solid linear regression line and the dashed unbiased model line ... demonstrates the systematic bias in a model that results from dropping censored observations. The riskiest transactions end up being the most poorly explained!" Active or delisted homes "are like Wald's planes that didn't return; they're censored observations", and the mismatch makes offline backtested metrics differ from online ones. Their reframe: a binary "sold within a horizon" classification that can still use delisted and active listings once they pass the horizon. Real estate, not used goods (*inferred* transfer).
- *observed* (arXiv 2605.07087, Komiyama, Matsuda, Ishihara, "A Finite-Horizon Mixture Cure Model with Application to Online Flea Market Data", submitted 2026-05-08, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07087): conventional cure models "rely on event occurrence over an infinite horizon, introducing untestable assumptions"; the proposed model estimates the event "within a finite time horizon" and is applied to transaction data from Mercari, finding it "identifies different significant variables compared to the conventional model". This is the one published time-to-sale model on a used-goods marketplace I found; I did not read its fitted numbers.
- *observed* (arXiv 2103.09763, Candes, Lei, Ren, "Conformalized Survival Analysis", 2021-03-17, https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09763): wraps any survival model to give "calibrated, covariate-dependent lower predictive bounds on survival times"; with Type I right-censoring and exogenous censoring times the bounds have finite-sample coverage, and under conditionally independent censoring a doubly robust property holds. The bound is a lower bound (for "at least N days"), not a two-sided range.
- *asserted* (search snippet, unopened): a used-car time-to-sale study uses delisting as the sale proxy on Craigslist-like ads and combines Cox with random survival forests. Delisting is an imperfect sale signal (Poshmark's lack of public final-sale dates is reported by a scraper vendor, *asserted*, unopened).
- *inferred*: for the spec's "days to sell" the options are (a) a Kaplan-Meier or discrete-time hazard per group and price-to-median ratio, where unsold items stay in the risk set until their end date, or (b) the finite-horizon framing ("P(sold within 7, 30 days)"), which matches the plan objectives in [price.md](../../../spec/features/price.md) line 68 to 70 ("sale within 7 days", "within 30 days"). The calibration check for a survival model is a calibration curve of predicted P(sold by day d) against the observed share among comparable items, with censoring handled by the Kaplan-Meier estimate inside each bin (*inferred*; scikit-survival and lifelines provide the estimators, not opened).
- Note on the queue idea at [pricing-in-context.md](../ben/pricing-in-context.md) line 51: it can supply the covariate (rank of your price among active asks) to a survival model, then be calibrated against actual outcomes (*inferred*).

## 7. Backtesting on sold history without leakage

- *observed* (scikit-learn docs, `TimeSeriesSplit`, https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit.html): it "provides train/test indices to split time-ordered data, where other cross-validation methods are inappropriate, as they would lead to training on future" data; `gap` (added in 0.24) is the "number of samples to exclude from the end of each train set before the test set".
- *observed* (Opendoor page above): dropping unsold or censored cases makes offline metrics disagree with online ones.
- *observed* (Underpriced AI page above): the evaluation excludes cases where the answer is mechanically tied to the ask: it grades headline accuracy only on sales where the seller chose their own price and shows the anchored 3% figure separately as "flattering by construction". It also measures coverage separately for ranges "priced since the range was rebuilt" (75.3% on 81) versus all older ranges (48.3% on 236), i.e. it reports coverage per model version.
- *observed* (FlipTip method, in [build-plan.md](../ben/build-plan.md) line 203): first photo only, tested sale removed from its own comps, Best Offer sales excluded.
- *inferred*, leakage routes specific to this design, each testable:
  1. Comps from after the sale date used to price it (fix: for each test sale, comps limited to sales strictly before it, with a gap of a few days).
  2. The tested sale, or a relist or duplicate of the same item, inside its own comps or the calibration set.
  3. Calibration residuals from the same period as the test period (fix: calibrate on the window before the test window, as in `TimeSeriesSplit`; the exchangeability test in crepes `martingales` can warn when old calibration data no longer matches).
  4. Adjustment factors (condition, drivers, site ratios) learned on the full history before the split.
  5. Sold price capped by the ask (Underpriced AI's point): a sale cannot exceed its list price, so grading on seller-chosen asks understates how often a range is too high.
  6. Survivorship: only sold items have a price, so unsold items need the censoring treatment in section 5, and a price range conditional on selling is a different target from one for all listings.
- *inferred*, procedure that fits the sources: rolling-origin evaluation. For each month m: fit adjustments and quantile heads on data before m minus a gap; calibrate conformal on a window just before m; score every sale in m on coverage (overall and per group), width, MdAPE, and for days to sell, calibration of P(sold by d) with censoring. Report coverage with a binomial interval, as the Underpriced AI page's 81 sales and group sizes of 9 to 78 show how wide that interval is.
