# Measuring whether the brand matters: sources for A11

Researched 2026-11-15 by Nate (Claude Code adaptation lead; abstracts read by the lead through Exa search). Feature: price.

For measure A11 in [spec/engines/adaptation.md](../../../spec/engines/adaptation.md#a11-whether-the-brand-matters). Each claim is marked *observed* (read in the source's abstract or text today), *asserted* (the source says it, not checked further) or *inferred* (ours). None of these sources studied secondhand marketplaces at scale; the method is borrowed and the borrowing is ours.

| Source | What it did | What it shows | How far it supports A11 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rosen (1974), "Hedonic Prices and Implicit Markets: Product Differentiation in Pure Competition", JPE 82(1):34-55, [doi:10.1086/260169](https://doi.org/10.1086/260169) | theory of goods priced by their characteristics | a good's price can be read as implicit prices of its characteristics (*asserted*: citation and title read, no abstract on the index page) | the frame: hold the characteristics, and what is left of the price gap is attributed to what was not held, here the brand (*inferred*) |
| Ailawadi, Lehmann and Neslin (2003), "Revenue Premium as an Outcome Measure of Brand Equity", Journal of Marketing 67(4):1-17, [doi:10.1509/jmkg.67.4.1.18688](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.67.4.1.18688) | packaged-goods categories; a brand's revenue against a private label's | the revenue premium over a private label is "a simple, objective, and managerially useful product-market measure of brand equity", reliable, tracks real changes, correlates with other equity measures (*observed*, abstract) | the closest established measure: a premium over a no-name alternative in the same category. A11 uses price, not revenue, because we cannot see quantities sold (*inferred*) |
| Bronnenberg, Dubé, Gentzkow and Shapiro (2015), "Do Pharmacists Buy Bayer? Informed Shoppers and the Brand Premium", QJE 130(4):1669-1726, [doi:10.1093/qje/qjv024](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjv024) | purchases in physically homogeneous categories; 50 health and 241 food and drink categories | pharmacists choose national brands of headache remedies 9% of the time against 26% for the average consumer; chefs give 12 points less of pantry-staple purchases to national brands; misinformation explains "a sizable share" of the brand premium for health products and "a much smaller share" for most food and drink (*observed*, abstract) | a brand premium is not all quality. Where items are physically the same, part of it is what buyers do not know. A11's off-brand finder speaks to exactly that buyer (*inferred*) |
| Steenkamp, van Heerde and Geyskens (2010), "What Makes Consumers Willing to Pay a Price Premium for National Brands over Private Labels?", JMR 47(6):1011-1024, [doi:10.1509/jmkr.47.6.1011](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.47.6.1011) | survey of 22,623 respondents in 23 countries, about 63 packaged-goods categories each | marketing and manufacturing factors move the premium people will pay through their perception of the brand's quality over the private label; willingness to pay falls as private labels mature (*observed*, abstract) | why "people care about the brand" is a perception we infer from prices, not observe (*inferred*) |
| Choudhary and Gaheer, "Resale Price Ratios and Brand Equity", IJSR 15(7) | 842 Grailed and Depop resale listings across fifteen fashion brands, plus a survey of 102 consumers | premium streetwear resold at 1.15 of retail on average against 0.71 for heritage brands (*observed*, abstract) | the only resale-market study found; small, one venue of low standing, and resale over retail rather than brand over matched off-brand. Weight: low (*inferred*) |
| Lu et al. (2025), International Journal of Sports Marketing and Sponsorship, [doi:10.1108/ijsms-12-2023-0248](https://doi.org/10.1108/ijsms-12-2023-0248) | 315,584 secondhand trades on POIZON, regression on co-branded sportswear | secondhand transaction prices used as a measure of a brand's value; partner-brand popularity lowers the co-branding premium (*observed*, abstract) | shows secondhand prices are used to measure brand value; not a matched-attribute design (*inferred*) |

## Not found

- A study of the brand premium at matched attributes on a general secondhand marketplace (eBay, Poshmark, Mercari, Depop, Vinted).
- A study relating a brand premium to time to sell on any resale market.
