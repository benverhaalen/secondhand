# Which depreciation and live-market proxies help price used goods when listed?
Researched 2026-11-15 by Ben (Codex). Feature: price.

## Question and decision

**Question:** Which measurable age, condition, release, listing-duration and market-state signals predict resale price or sale probability across clothing, sneakers, electronics, bags, home goods and toys, and how large are their effects?

**Decision fed:** *agent proposal* for additions to [money D1/D2/D3/D7/D10/D15](../../../spec/determinations/money.md), [market.py](../../../jobs/valuation/market.py), [comps.ts](../../../app/src/engines/valuation/comps.ts), and the next dataset under the [backtest plan](../../../spec/plans/backtest/plan.md) and [datasets](../../../spec/plans/backtest/datasets.md). No implementation or settled decision changes here.

**Draft from memory. asserted:** Ordinary goods lose value fastest initially, then more slowly; better condition commands a premium. Hype premiums fade after release. Old listings become harder to sell. Supply relative to recent sales should add information beyond sold comps. A small dated used-goods panel should test these mechanisms better than deadstock StockX.

## What changed

| Draft | Finding after searches for and against it |
|---|---|
| Age causes depreciation | *Observed*, S1: computer specifications explain much of the apparent age curve. *Inferred*: distinguish physical wear, technological vintage and calendar-market change. |
| Hype premiums fade | *Observed*, S3: StockX describes recovery after an initial fall. *Asserted*, S4: a recent collector reports modest, nonmonotonic decay. One universal negative slope is unsupported. |
| Older listings mean worse goods | *Observed*, S7: better-condition electronics sometimes take longer to sell controlling for price. *Inferred*: duration contains selection and seller patience as well as possible stigma. |
| Market proxies beat comps | *Observed*, S9: housing tightness improves forecasts beyond price history, with different results by country. *Inferred*: plausible mechanism, still unproved incremental lift for our six categories. |
| A small snapshot is enough | *Observed*, S6: retrospectively collected Mercari attributes can differ from listing-time attributes. *Inferred*: repeated, timestamped observations and unsold outcomes are essential. |

## Evidence, exact numbers and transfer limits

All sources below were opened and checked on **2026-11-15**. Locators identify the inspected passage; quoted fragments preserve exact reported numbers. Each reference's job and proposed transfer follow its finding.

**S1. Electronics; evidence and implementation mechanism.** *Observed*: Antonopoulos and Sakellaris, March 2011, [author-uploaded full paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227611535_Estimating_computer_depreciation_using_online_auction_data), p.183, abstract, text lines 97–114: four-year used PCs are “almost one tenth” of new; the initial new-to-used loss is “20–25 percentage points.” Sections 5–7 distinguish technical specifications from physical age. *Inferred*: use generation/specifications alongside wear; these old Dell results cannot set today's phone depreciation or a grade multiplier.

**S2. Durables; mechanism with an external-category comparison.** *Observed*: Hulten–Wykoff, 1981, [The Measurement of Economic Depreciation](https://econweb.umd.edu/~hulten/webpagefiles/original%20hulten-wykoff%20economic%20depreciation%20study.pdf), pp.94–96, lines 339–420: approximately geometric profiles; autos “33 percent” annually. Many other asset rates were imputed from service lives, not directly estimated. P.91 discusses surviving-asset selection. *Inferred*: borrow flexible age-price estimation and selection checks; do not transplant business-capital rates to household furniture or appliances. A defensible home-goods age/condition coefficient remains missing.

**S3. Sneakers; platform evidence against monotone decay.** *Observed*: [StockX Buyer's Guide](https://stockx.com/news/the-stockx-data-guide-to-resale/), March 21, 2019, updated February 15, 2022; “Retail Multiples,” lines 41–43: “27.7%” below retail. “Resale over time,” lines 97–110: hyped releases initially fall, then recover; ordinary Ultraboost lacks that pattern. *Inferred*: transfer release-aligned, model-specific curves and separate hype from ordinary stock. The platform supplies no universal numerical decay coefficient here.

**S4. Sneakers; weaker quantitative comparison.** *Asserted*: [SneakerPulse](https://www.sneakers-pulse.com/tools/best-time-to-sell), “By model”/“How this is measured,” lines 37–46, 80–82, reports “838 releases” and “4.5%,” “7.4%,” “6.6%” below launch at weeks 4/12/26. Launch is the busiest week within eight weeks after first reaching 20 weekly sales, not official release. *Observed*: another returned cached version showed 1,287 releases and −10.0/−14.1/−18.7%; underlying sales were not reproduced. *Inferred*: this first-party collector's changing definition demonstrates sensitivity; use its normalization as an experiment, not its numbers as engine priors.

**S5. Bags; interaction evidence.** *Asserted*: Yuan, Deily and Yao, 2022, [publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/poms.13572), lines 30–33, reports quiet-logo premiums of “$161–$174,” “17%–18%” of retail, and faster premium erosion with worsening condition. Full coefficient tables were inaccessible. *Inferred*: condition × design matters; this is a logo premium, not a measured excellent-to-good discount. Transfer the interaction test, not those dollars.

**S6. Clothing duration; survival mechanism.** *Observed*: Komiyama et al., [May 8, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07087v1), §4.1/Table 3, lines 251–284: “98,258” T-shirt listings, “66,298” completed transactions, “32.5%” censored; survival levels near 30%. Listings date from 2020; attributes were extracted June 2023, with artificial January 2021 censoring. Completion includes shipping/receipt/review; durations ≤one day are excluded. *Inferred*: supports censored duration modeling, not a causal age penalty or purchase-time hazard. Its post-listing attribute assumption and random split should not replace our prospective freeze.

**S7. Electronics; counterevidence and controls.** *Observed*: Ghose, June 2009, [full paper](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~aghose/MISQtpas.pdf), p.274, lines 748–801: log-condition coefficients in column 2 are 0.035 for cameras, 0.1 for audio players, 0.03 for laptops; better condition is associated with longer sale time controlling for price, competition and seller characteristics. *Inferred*: these are elasticities, not percentage discounts per condition grade. Borrow product/seller controls; reject “long-listed means bad” as an automatic inference.

**S8. Clothing; retail-retention evidence.** *Observed*: [PwC, March 2024](https://www.pwc.co.uk/issues/esg/pdf/circular-fashion-making-resale-reality.pdf), printed p.13, lines 349–389: over 20 items/500 datapoints, most retain “c.25-40%”; Primark “c.56%.” Analysts compared websites and charity shops. *Inferred*: useful ordinary-fashion scale comparison; neither a longitudinal depreciation curve nor verified transaction-only hedonic evidence. Low retail denominators can increase retention ratios without greater demand.

**S9. Market state; forecasting mechanism.** *Observed*: Carrillo–de Wit–Larson, [August 2012 working paper](https://www2.gwu.edu/~iiep/assets/docs/papers/Carrillo_IIEPWP2012-11.pdf), Table 3, PDF p.42, lines 1145–1163: Fairfax price-history-only forecast RMSE “0.090”; adding bargaining power and sale probability gives “0.066.” Netherlands: “0.0149” → “0.0137”; sale probability alone gives “0.0129.” §4, pp.25–26: recursive forecasts; bargaining power alone does not improve the Dutch forecast. *Inferred*: evidence for information beyond lagged prices, not beyond item-level sold comps in consumer goods. Borrow the paired forecast comparison, not housing coefficients.

**S10. Toys; counterexample.** *Observed*: Dobrynskaya–Kishilova, [April 2018 full preprint mirror](https://product-sales-creator.com/SSRN-id3291456.pdf?x80413=), abstract p.1: “11%” nominal, “8%” real average returns, 1987–2015; §4, printed p.10: only new sets, contents sealed, box condition variable. *Inferred*: retirement/scarcity can reverse depreciation. This cannot price played-with toys or missing LEGO pieces; collect completeness and sealed status separately.

**S11. Listing stigma; external causal evidence.** *Observed*: Tucker–Zhang–Zhu, [March 21, 2012 paper](https://ide.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/2012.03_Tucker_Zhang_Zhu_Days-on-Market-and-Homes-Sales_316.pdf), p.1, abstract lines 5–16: prohibition on resetting days-on-market reduced Massachusetts prices by “$16,000” relative to Rhode Island. *Inferred*: visibility of cumulative age can matter; policy exposure is not one extra listing day. Preserve relist history; learn goods-specific effects.

**S12. Concurrent competition; goods mechanism.** *Observed*: Anwar–McMillan–Zheng, [May 2004 revised paper](https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/mic/papers/0505/0505001.pdf), p.3, lines 116–117: cross-bidders pay “91 percent” of non-cross-bidders' price. *Inferred*: competing offers matter; bidder selection prevents interpreting the difference as the effect of increasing supply. Transfer simultaneous substitute-set measurement.

*Inferred*: prospectively retain identity/variant, condition/completeness, release/manufacture evidence, initial and edited asks, shipping, first-seen/last-seen times, competing snapshots and verified sale/cancellation outcomes. ## Search and access limits

*Observed*: searched hedonic/depreciation, condition-grade resale, release-premium decay, listing-age hazards/stigma and concurrent supply; countersearches covered appreciation, adverse selection and tightness beyond price history. Searched eBay, Mercari, StockX, Grailed, ThredUp and Poshmark. [ThredUp 2025](https://www.thredup.com/resale/2025) opened; its PDF exceeded the browser's size limit. [Poshmark's June 2026 release](https://blog.poshmark.com/2026/06/09/59714/amp/) reports daily-updated seven-day fast-selling brand/category signals, without predictive coefficients. NBER and Lehigh full-text routes returned 403; publisher abstracts remained reachable. No defensible grade-price curves for all six categories, ordinary home/toy age curves, or incremental consumer-goods price lift from each live-market proxy were established. These are measured evidence gaps, not claims that the literature is absent.
