Researched 2026-11-14 by Ben (Claude Code research helper). Feature: list.

Marks: *observed* (opened today, the page says so), *asserted* (a source's claim, memory or seller folklore), *inferred* (my reasoning), *agent proposal* (my suggestion).

Source codes: **HTS** = [howtosell.grailed.com](https://howtosell.grailed.com/), Grailed's seller guide, undated and partly stale. **SPT** = [Grailed Engineering, Seller Pro-Tips](https://medium.com/grailed-engineering/seller-pro-tips-c6ea7eb2ca3c), undated, likely several years old. **FT** = [Flipper Tools](https://flippertools.com/articles/how-to-sell-on-grailed), updated 2026-09-24, a third-party guide that lists the Grailed Help articles it draws on. ## 1. Short answer

1. **Designer, category and size first.** Grailed is browsed by designer and size. A wrong designer or size hides the listing from the people who would pay for it (*inferred*; the filters are *asserted* from memory).
2. **The 10% price drop.** A drop of 10% or more moves the listing to the top of the feed and notifies everyone who favorited it (*observed*, HTS).
3. **Bump every 7 days.** Free. After 30 days a bump needs a 10% drop (*observed*, HTS).
4. **Offers bind.** Accepting a binding offer makes a contract (*observed*, [Terms](https://www.grailed.com/about/terms), effective 2026-01-27). Sellers can also send an offer to the last 100 people who favorited (*observed*, HTS).
5. **The $120 fee step.** Since 2026-05-20 commission is 6% under $120 (minimum $1.99) and 9% from $120 (*observed*, [Grailed fee page](https://support.grailed.com/hc/en-us/articles/30282580172045-What-are-the-fees)). A sale at $120 to $123 nets less than a sale at $119 (*inferred*, section 4).
6. **Tag and label photos.** Grailed asks for collar tag and wash tag photos (*observed*, HTS), and listings are reviewed for authenticity before they go live (*asserted*, FT).
7. **Keep it short.** Grailed's own data: titles of 7 words or fewer, descriptions of 20 to 60 words and fewer than 10 photos sell faster (*observed*, SPT).
8. **Grailed Labels.** US to US orders must use one (*asserted*, FT). Commission is then on the item price only (*observed*, fee page) and the buyer pays the label.

**Biggest difference from the other platforms:** Grailed is a fashion-only market sorted by designer, with public sold prices. It has no category for a phone, a couch or a blender (*asserted*), and its Terms forbid third-party tools that touch the site without written consent (*observed*, Terms section 12). So the adapter is narrow and the post step needs the user's hand.

## 6. Title and description conventions

**Formula** (*agent proposal*, from Grailed's advice to use the item's original name, *observed*, HTS):

`{Brand or line} {original model name} {season or year} {color} {size}`

- Seven words or fewer. Such titles "tend to do significantly better than those with over 10 words" (*observed*, SPT).
- Use the real model name and season. Grailed's example is "Black Metallic Jordan 5 2001" (*observed*, HTS).
- The designer shows above the title, but sellers repeat the brand because the title is what shows in shares and search engines (*asserted*, folklore).
- Leave out emoji, "RARE", "GRAIL" and other brands' names. Buyers read brand stuffing as a red flag (*asserted*, memory).

**Clothing example** (*agent proposal*): designer `Rick Owens`, title `Rick Owens Geobasket Black Milk FW14 43` (7 words, 39 characters).

**Non-clothing example** (*agent proposal*): Grailed has no non-fashion categories, so the nearest case is an accessory. Designer `Chrome Hearts`, title `Chrome Hearts Cemetery Cross Trucker Hat Black` (7 words, 46 characters).

**Description template** (fields are Grailed's, *observed*, HTS; order is an *agent proposal*):

```
{One or two sentences: what it is, season, how it fits}
Size: {tag size}
Measurements: pit to pit {x} in, length {x} in, sleeve {x} in
Condition: {n}/10. {every flaw, plainly}
Retail: ${x}
Ships next day with tracking.
#tag1 #tag2 #tag3
```

Length: 20 to 60 words, the range that "historically led to the highest proportion of listings sold" (*observed*, SPT). Tone: plain and factual. Buyers here compare measurements across seasons, so always give them (*asserted*, FT). Search reads title, designer, description and hashtags; designer, category, size, condition, price and location are filters (*asserted*, memory).

## 8. Photos

**What Grailed asks for** (*observed*, HTS): indirect natural light; a simple background such as a nail on a solid wall or a clean floor; fabric, silhouette, collar tag and wash tag; fit photos cropped at the neck.

**How many:** "listings that have too many (10+) photos take longer to sell", and quality beats count (*observed*, SPT; a correlation, and old).

**Authentication** (*asserted*, FT): listings are checked digitally before going live. High-risk items get human review by Grailed or vetted partners, the rest get machine moderation. Grailed may ask for extra photos and can reject any listing. Passing listings can show a "Grailed Verified" card. Reviewers want the neck tag, care and content labels, serial or style codes, hardware and stitching close-ups, front, back and a hanger or body shot. Shoes: insole, outsole, box label.

**Norms and rules** (*asserted*, memory; Grailed's photo policy page could not be opened): the cover is a hanger shot on a plain wall or a flat lay on a clean floor. Own photos only; stock and retail images get listings pulled. Moderators can ask for a tagged photo (the item with a handwritten username and date).

**AI images:** the Terms have no rule on AI-generated or AI-edited photos (*observed*; they mention AI only as a banned way to pull data). No Grailed photo tool or listing AI was found (*asserted*).

**Photo list** (*agent proposal*): 6 to 9 photos. Order: front, back, neck tag, wash and size tag, style code or hardware close-up, each flaw, fit pic if the user has one. Shoes: side, top, outsole, insole, size tag, box label. Go past 9 only for more flaws.

### Under Ben's touch-up rule

Grailed is the easiest platform for the rule. Its own advice is a real item on a plain wall or floor in daylight (*observed*, HTS). White cut-out backgrounds are not the norm and can read as a stock photo (*inferred*).

- **Meet at capture:** guide the user to a plain wall with a hanger or a clean floor, near a window, no flash. Make the neck tag and wash tag required shots, since they are the authentication evidence (*inferred*).
- **Edit:** exposure, white balance, straighten, crop. Nothing else.
- **Crop:** cover at 4:5 portrait, 1600 x 2000 px, item centered with a small margin (*agent proposal*; the 4:5 feed crop is *asserted* and should be checked on a live listing). Detail shots keep the same ratio.
- **What the rule stops:** almost nothing sellers here rely on. Black garments are common and auto-exposure turns them grey, so hold blacks. Do not lift faded black back to black, smooth leather creases or remove stains; name the fading in the description (*inferred*).

**How sellers price** (*observed*, HTS): check sold listings, not retail. Resale can be 3x retail or 20% of it.

**How haggling goes** (*observed*, SPT): listings take around 7 to 8 offers on average before a price is agreed, but over half sell with 3 or fewer.

**Seller folklore** (*asserted*, [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/platform-specific-guides/how-to-sell-on-grailed), updated 2026-09-02): many first offers land at 40 to 50% of asking, and sellers price 15 to 20% above the lowest price they would take.

**Room for offers** (*agent proposal*): list at target divided by 0.85. That leaves one 10% drop and a small offer discount, which fits Grailed's figure of 2 or 3 drops before a typical sale. Keep the expected sale price out of the $120 to $123 band.

## 12. The algorithm

What Grailed has said (*observed*, HTS): a bump or a 10% drop puts the listing at the top of the feed, and drops notify favoriters. That is the only ranking statement I could read. The rest is data or folklore.

| Factor | Evidence | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| Recency of bump or 10% drop | Grailed's own page | *observed* (HTS) |
| Designer, category and size | Filters and designer follows decide who sees the listing at all | *inferred* |
| Section | A mass-market brand lands in Core, away from people browsing Grails. My memory said the sections were retired; Grailed Help still describes them | *asserted* (FT); effect is *inferred* |
| Favorites | Each favorite is a future notification target for drops and offers | *observed* (HTS); ranking effect unknown |
| Time of day | Most sales happen from 2 to 4 PM and from 8 to 10 PM Eastern, so bump or drop just before | *observed* (SPT); timing rule is *inferred* |
| Seller history | Each earlier sale lowers average time to sale by over 3 hours | *observed* (SPT) |
| Default sort | A mixed "Default" order, with Trending, New, Low Price and High Price as options | *asserted* (memory) |

**Relist versus edit** (*inferred*): a relist resets the date but loses the favorites, which are the notification list. A 10% drop already gives the top-of-feed effect, so prefer drops and bumps. Relist only a listing with no favorites. Grailed has a new "Listing Deletion" help page that I could not read.

## 15. Adapter rules (agent proposal)

1. **Gate.** Post only clothing, shoes, bags, jewelry or accessories whose brand maps to a Grailed designer. Refuse everything else, and refuse watches from major luxury brands (sections 13 and 14).
2. **Warn** under $33 (minimum fee) and for fast fashion, mall brands, unbranded and custom pieces, which Grailed can remove (sections 4 and 13).
3. **Title.** `{Brand or line} {model name} {season or year} {color} {size}`, at most 7 words and 60 characters, no emoji, no other brands (section 6).
4. **Designer.** One designer; up to 3 only for real collaborations (section 5).
5. **Tags.** 3 to 6 hashtags at the end of the description (section 7).
6. **Description.** The section 6 template, 20 to 60 words, with measurements and every flaw.
7. **Photos.** 6 to 9, in the section 8 order. Cover 4:5 at 1600 x 2000 px. Capture on a plain wall or floor in daylight. Tag shots are required.
8. **Touch-up.** Light, color, straighten and crop only. Hold blacks. Never clean up wear (section 8).
9. **Price.** List at target divided by 0.85. If the expected sale price falls between $120 and $123, set the floor at $119 (sections 4 and 9).
10. **Offers.** On. Suggest a floor at target, and suggest declining offers under 70% of asking (section 9).
11. **Refresh.** Remind the user to bump every 7 days, timed for 2 PM or 8 PM Eastern. At day 14 with favorites, suggest an offer to likers. From day 30, suggest a 10% drop, since bumps then need one. Never script these (sections 10, 12 and 13).
12. **Shipping.** Grailed Label; no weight or shipping price to enter for US buyers. Warn at 20 lb or more. US only by default; international needs a seller-set rate and a tap. Tell the user that sales over $750 may get signature confirmation. Ship within 2 days (section 11).
13. **Wait for review.** Show the listing as "in review" until Grailed publishes it (section 8).
14. **Never** relist a listing that has favorites (section 12).
15. **Do not edit** a listing while it has a live offer; the edit voids the offer (section 9).

**Human taps, not automatic:** publishing, every bump, accepting or countering an offer (it binds, and a counter voids the buyer's offer), every price drop, offers to likers, turning on Smart Pricing, any authenticity statement, offering free shipping, opening international shipping, deleting a listing.

## 16. Reading Grailed for search and price

**What a listing exposes** (*observed* in our earlier note [comps-sources-live.md](../../../price/ben/comps-sources-live.md), which read two Apify actors over Grailed's search backend; I could not re-open the actor pages today):

| Field | Active listing | Sold listing |
|---|---|---|
| Asking price | Yes | Yes (last asking price) |
| Price drop history (`price_drops`) | Yes | Yes |
| Sold price | Not applicable | Yes, the final price |
| Sold date | Not applicable | In one actor, missing in the other |
| Created date | Yes | Yes |
| Size, designers, condition, category | Yes | Yes |
| Seller location | Yes (*asserted*, memory) | Yes (*asserted*, memory) |

**Are sold prices public?** Yes. Grailed tells sellers to check sold listings before pricing, and listing pages carry a Price Comparison button (*observed*, HTS). Sold listings can be filtered on the site (*asserted*, memory).

**How reliable are they as comps?**

- The sold price is the final price, so it reflects accepted offers. eBay hides the accepted Best Offer amount (*observed* in comps-sources-live.md). This is Grailed's main advantage as a comp source (*inferred*).
- The price-drop trail shows how far a seller had to fall, which is a direct read of demand (*inferred*).
- Weak points (*inferred*): shipping is not in the sold price; refunded sales may still show as sold; fakes that sold cheap pull the low end down; price moves a lot with size, so comps need the same size band; volume is thin outside menswear.

**Weight Grailed above eBay for** (*agent proposal*): `designer_clothing`, `streetwear`, designer and archive footwear inside `sneakers` and `shoes_other`, and high-end `vintage_clothing` (band tees, archive denim). Weight the two equally for `outdoor_apparel` and designer `accessories`. Weight eBay above Grailed for `mens_mainstream`, `fan_apparel`, `womens_mall_brands` and everything Grailed blocks.

**The Terms problem** (*observed*, Terms section 12): Grailed forbids scrapers and AI systems that pull data, and our search and price route calls Grailed's search backend. How we still build it (*agent proposal*): keep reads low-volume and triggered by a user action, label the comps as research-grade in the demo, and for a product ask Grailed or GOAT Group for a data agreement.
