# What do existing reseller and marketplace seller tools show a seller about earnings, profit and goals, and when do they notify, and what does published evidence say about batching notifications into digests versus sending each one at once?
Researched 2026-11-14 by Nate (Claude Code retriever, Sonnet 5.5). Feature: insights (filed under today because research folders must match a features/ slug).

Mark key: *observed* = I opened the page text and it says so (URL, page date given when the page has one). *asserted* = source claims it, or memory, or a search summary. *inferred* = my reasoning. "Unopened" = link seen in a search result, not opened.

## 1. Crosslisters: which numbers, and cost of goods

- Vendoo. *Observed* (https://help.vendoo.co/en/articles/6260294-vendoo-business-analytics, page dated July 9, 2026): "You will see your total revenue and profit for the date selected, in addition to a summary of sold items, listed items, and average sale price (ASP) with a comparison to the prior period." The same page lists a breakdown by marketplace, in-person and non-integrated sales, top categories, sub-categories and brands, analytics by custom label, and says it can "help you to establish goals". It names no sell-through or days-to-sell figure.
- Vendoo cost of goods. *Observed* (Vendoo profit help pages, July 9, 2026): "Vendoo uses your cost of goods (COG), sale price, marketplace fees, and shipping fees to instantly calculate your profit and revenue." Details are added when an item is marked sold or a sale is detected; there is a CSV inventory report. COG is typed by the seller.
- Sell The Flip (a crosslister not on the brief's list, included because it states its formula). *Observed* (https://selltheflip.com/docs/insights, undated): cards for Total revenue, Total profit ("Sale price minus fees, minus shipping you paid, minus what the item cost you"), Avg sale price, Items sold, New listings, Median days to sell, each compared to the prior period. "We cannot know what you paid for an item, so until you tell us, item cost is treated as zero and your profit reads higher than it really is", with a banner and an "Add item costs" button. Fees from eBay are actual; for every other marketplace they are estimates with an "est. fees" badge the seller can overwrite. Facebook sales arrive through a browser extension.
- Crosslist. *Observed* (https://crosslist.com/, fetched 2026-11-14, undated): feature blurb "Sales analytics: Visualize sales, revenue, and profit in one clear dashboard". No field list found.
- OneShop. *Observed* (https://www.oneshop.com/, fetched 2026-11-14, undated): "Check a unified graph of your sales, set monthly goals, and see how you stack up against other sellers using OneShop." This is the only crosslister found with a stated goal feature and a peer comparison.
- List Perfectly. *Observed* (https://listperfectly.com/, fetched 2026-11-14, undated): "Management & Analytics ... get powerful analytics" and "Sales detection, notification, and delist from all marketplaces." No metric list found.
- Flyp. *Observed* (https://www.joinflyp.com/, fetched 2026-11-14): the word "Analytics" appears in a feature list next to "All-in-one orders dashboard". No metric list found.
- Closo, Nifty: no first-party analytics page reached (see Not found).
- Against the existing notes: confirms [crosslister-teardown.md](../../cross-cutting/nate/crosslister-teardown.md) line 89 (Analytics row: Y for Vendoo, List Perfectly, Crosslist, Flyp; "?" for OneShop and Closo) and extends it: OneShop is now *observed* to advertise a sales graph and monthly goals, so that "?" can become Y. Extends [analytics-and-recommendations.md](../../data/nate/analytics-and-recommendations.md) lines 141-150 (not-found list) with the Vendoo and Sell The Flip formulas. Consistent with [fees-and-taxes.md](../../price/nate/fees-and-taxes.md) line 77 and line 108: the tools that show "profit" all depend on a typed cost; the marketplaces' own "keep" is before cost.

## 2. Marketplaces: which numbers

- eBay Seller Hub, Performance. *Observed* (https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/seller-hub?id=4095, undated, via Exa fetch): Performance gives "detailed information on sales, selling costs as a percentage of sales, traffic, buyer traffic source, and more". The Sales report gives "item sales, shipping and handling and government taxes and fees", the share from Promoted Listings, and "the amount and the percentage of your selling cost that came from eBay fees and shipping labels", with a prior-period comparison; a "Download listings sales report" exists. No cost-of-goods or profit field appears on that page.
- eBay Seller Hub, extra. *Observed* (https://export.ebay.com/en/services-tools/seller-hub/monitoring-your-business-in-seller-hub/, undated): Buyer insights (distinct buyers, one-time and repeat buyer percentages), Listings insights filtered by "Sold via advertising, Sold via Best offer, and Sold via Seller initiated offer", Traffic with impressions, page views, quantity sold, CTR and "Sales conversion rate (the quantity sold divided by the total page views ...)". Sell-through and days-to-sell are not in that list. A third-party blog (https://safebay.store/blog/ebay-sales-analytics, 2026-11-08) puts sell-through under the Research tab, "sold items divided by active listings, multiplied by 100" (*asserted*, a blog). That is formula one of the two in [analytics-and-recommendations.md](../../data/nate/analytics-and-recommendations.md) lines 31-32. Confirms line 26 and line 76 (traffic report fields). My direct fetches of ebay.com help pages returned a verification stub (780 bytes), so these came through Exa.
- Mercari. *Observed* (https://www.mercari.com/us/help_center/article/478/, undated): Seller Stats "Key metrics" are "On sale: Number of items you have on sale" and "Sold: Number of items you sold since the day you started", with trend icons. Extends [analytics-and-recommendations.md](../../data/nate/analytics-and-recommendations.md) lines 79-82 (Mercari not found). Only the highlight was returned, so other fields on that page may exist. Net proceeds sit in the Gross Sales Report with "No item cost" per [fees-and-taxes.md](../../price/nate/fees-and-taxes.md) line 133.
- Depop. *Observed* (https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360019016817-Shop-Stats, undated): Shop Stats shows Earnings ("the amount you've received from sales after deducting the transaction and boosting fees", "before refunds"), Items sold, and, iOS only, "Potential revenue" (current price of all live listings, "before deducting transaction fees ...") and Listings posted. "All data is historical ... If you make sales today for example, they'll show in your stats tomorrow." Data is in UTC. Extends line 80-ish of the analytics note (Depop not found). Interesting for the spec: Depop's own screen has an "earnings after fees" and a "potential revenue before fees" pair, close to the "You kept" and "Could earn" pair in [insights.md](../../../spec/features/insights.md) lines 12 and 29, except Depop's potential is before fees.
- Poshmark. *Observed* (https://blog.poshmark.com/2026/04/29/59472/, 2026-04-29): "we introduced Listing Metrics and updated Seller Stats"; Seller Stats at "Seller Tools > Posh Stats"; listing impressions "soon" in grid view. An older third-party post (https://tools.oneshop.com/blog/closet-insights-poshmark, 2022-01-22, *asserted*) lists Closet Insights charts: Sales Summary and Comparison, Brand Insights, Sales Insights (average sale price, listings sold), Inventory Summary, Listing Insights. Poshmark's own support article (https://support.poshmark.com/s/article/My-Closet-Insights) returned only a loading shell. Confirms [analytics-and-recommendations.md](../../data/nate/analytics-and-recommendations.md) line 78.
- Cost of goods on every marketplace screen above: none shows a cost field in the text I read. *Inferred* from the absence in the pages read, not proven.
- Goals: only OneShop (monthly goals) and Vendoo ("help you to establish goals") mention goals. No marketplace page read mentions seller goals. [insights.md](../../../spec/features/insights.md) line 4 and lines 135-138 (a goal feature) therefore has few direct precedents in this set.

## 3. How each notifies

- Seller-tool notifications per event: List Perfectly "Sales detection, notification, and delist" (*observed*, homepage above). The tier limit on detection alerts at [crosslister-teardown.md](../../cross-cutting/nate/crosslister-teardown.md) line 68 (2025-09-11) stands unchecked by me.
- Vendoo polls about every 10 minutes ([crosslister-teardown.md](../../cross-cutting/nate/crosslister-teardown.md) line 20, not rechecked). Sell The Flip checks Facebook "about every 30 mins while your browser is open" and eBay "every few hours" (*observed*, selltheflip.com above): sale detection latency is minutes to hours, so any "push at once" on a sale is bounded by that.
- Depop stats arrive the next day (*observed*, above), so a daily summary has no fresher earnings than that on Depop.
- Digest versus per-event settings for Vendoo, Flyp, OneShop, Crosslist, Closo, Poshmark, Mercari, Depop and eBay: not found in first-party text. eBay's notification page was not reached.
- Against the specs: [today.md](../../../spec/features/today.md) line 140 says batched notifications for sellers are untested; this search found no seller-tool evidence on either side, which agrees (*inferred*).

## 4. Evidence on batching versus immediate delivery

- Fitz et al. 2019, Computers in Human Behavior vol 101, pp 84-94, DOI 10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.016. *Observed* through the authors' Springer Nature post (Aug 01, 2019; the paper itself I did not read, doi.org gave only a redirect): "over 200 people residing in India", four groups, 2 weeks: as-usual alerts (control), no alerts (tray only), alerts once an hour on the hour, alerts three times a day (9am, 3pm, 9pm). The three-a-day group was happier, more productive, less stressed, more attentive and unlocked their phones less; hourly "had few benefits"; the no-alerts group did not differ on those but was "more, not less, anxious" and unlocked no less. No objective performance measure. N = 237 is *asserted* only (search summary). Applies to a general sample, not sellers, and the notifications were all alerts, not time-critical ones (*inferred*). Supports [insights.md](../../../spec/features/insights.md) line 94 (about 3 batches a day) as to count and also matches [psychology.md](../../cross-cutting/ben/psychology.md) lines 115-142 (batching justified by interruption cost). Extends it with the finding that hourly and none are worse than three.
- Ego depletion: [psychology.md](../../cross-cutting/ben/psychology.md) lines 115-142 and 313 already say it cannot be used (Hagger d=0.04, Vohs d=0.06); I did not need it and did not recheck.
- No study found comparing digest versus immediate for transactional or money alerts (offers, deadlines). See Not found.

## 5. Opt-out and disable rates

- Airship, "Mobile App Push Notification Benchmarks for 2026" (https://www.airship.com/mobile-app-push-notification-benchmarks-for-2026/, page copyright 2026). *Observed*: "681 billion interactions" analysed; opt-in is "a monthly average over 12 months"; "our Global Consumer Survey Report reveals that the top reason users opt out is because messages get sent 'too frequently.'"; top-decile senders (90th percentile) on iOS rose 15.71% to 187.8 pushes per user per month, Android 18.16% to 178.9; Media 359.3 (iOS) and 360.3 (Android) at the 90th percentile; Government 10th percentile 0.1 per user per month on iOS; apps at the 90th percentile for sends see "a 72% average increase in sessions per active user compared to their category average"; "Android and iOS opt-in rates to near parity". The opt-in percentages per category sit in tables I did not extract from the page text. Airship's own claim that more frequent sending lifts sessions cuts against its own survey on reasons for opting out; both are vendor statements (*inferred*).
- Braze, "How often do people opt out of push" (https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/opt-out-of-push-notifications-why-users-do-it.md, dated 2016-05-09). *Observed*: 14 Braze staff, 1,526 apps (109 per phone), opted out of 584, "a push opt-out rate of 38.3%" ("24.5% with Mr. 297 excluded"); the authors say the results "clearly aren't statistically significant". Reasons: 30% not personalized or relevant, 25% "too many push notifications", 25% default response to being asked, 20% no interest. To re-enable: 41.2% need evidence the messages would be valuable. Old (2016), n=14, staff of a messaging vendor: weak evidence (*inferred*).
- OneSignal. 2024 benchmarks page (https://onesignal.com/mobile-app-benchmarks-2024) and 2026 report page (https://onesignal.com/2026-state-of-customer-engagement-report) fetched; the 2024 page has per-category Android and iOS opt-in rate tables and the 2026 page only advertises "2026 push opt-in rates, session duration, churn rates, and 1/7/30-day retention benchmarks" behind a form. I did not extract per-category numbers. No OneSignal disable-rate number read.
- Not an opt-out rate for transactional pushes by any vendor: all three are marketing-heavy datasets (*inferred*).

## 6. Apple scheduled delivery and interruption levels

- *Observed* (Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Managing notifications, undated; https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications): delivery scheduling "lets people choose whether to receive notification alerts immediately or in a summary that's delivered at times they choose." Four interruption levels: Passive and Active do not override scheduled delivery; Time Sensitive and Critical do and break through Focus; only Critical overrides the Ring/Silent switch. Time Sensitive is for an event "happening now or will happen within an hour". "Never use the Time Sensitive interruption level to send a marketing notification." The system lets the person turn Time Sensitive off after the first one and re-evaluates periodically. "Build trust by accurately representing the urgency of each notification."
- *Observed* (Apple developer docs, `UNNotificationInterruptionLevel.timeSensitive`, copyright 2026, iOS 15.0 and later): "The system presents the notification immediately, lights up the screen, can play a sound, and breaks through system notification controls." It can "break through system controls such as Notification Summary and Focus. The user can turn off the ability for time sensitive notification interruptions."
- *Inferred*: the platform already lets the person pick summary versus immediate for the whole app, and Apple's own wording ties immediate delivery to events within an hour. That matches [today.md](../../../spec/features/today.md) line 119 (expiry push 2 hours before) as being outside the one-hour wording, which is a mismatch to read, not a ruling. Android equivalents not researched.
