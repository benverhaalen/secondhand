Researched 2026-11-15 by Ben (Codex, GPT-6). Feature: cross-cutting.

## Answer

Advertisers pay affiliates to acquire profitable demand while making most of the acquisition expense conditional on a completed sale. *(inferred from [EPN terms][epn-terms] and [Amazon terms][amazon-terms])* A 4% commission costs $1.32 on a $33 qualifying order; however, at 25% incrementality it costs $5.28 per additional order, because four credited orders produce only one additional order. *(inferred: `33 × .04 / .25`; 25% is an assumption)* Retail advertising benchmarks below imply materially higher attributed acquisition expense, but their audiences, baskets and attribution methods differ, so they do not establish a bargain for a resale marketplace. *(inferred)* A marketplace should pay us more for discovering an item or bringing a new buyer than for inserting a link after the buyer already chose that marketplace, provided a controlled test demonstrates the difference. *(inferred)* **eBay is the strongest documented starting point; a blanket 3% resale-network commission is unsupported, and no program's published rate establishes this app's approval or settled earnings.** *(inferred; evidence below)*

### 1. Pay for a sale versus pay for attention

Define `r = commission / eligible order value`, `A = order value`, `q = fraction of credited orders that are additional`. Affiliate attributed cost/order is `rA`; incremental cost/order is `rA/q`. For click advertising, attributed cost/order is `CPC / purchase-conversion rate`; for display it is `CPM / (1,000 × click-through rate × purchase-conversion rate)` if counting click conversions. These formulas omit advertiser network/agency charges, creative costs and view-through attribution. Acquiring an order is also different from acquiring a **new customer**, who can have repeat value. *(inferred definitions)*

The evidence supports **heterogeneity**, not a single industry haircut. Coupons can reduce abandonment or win a buyer from another merchant; they can also attach a discount and commission to a purchase already chosen. An advertiser can exclude coupon/cashback channels, require unique approved codes, restrict brand bidding, pay less for existing customers or pay more for new customers to concentrate spend on expected added contribution. ThredUp's 15% new versus 1% returning rate demonstrates this distinction directly; the future-dated Etsy policy separately shows channel exclusions but is not valid cutoff evidence. *(inferred mechanisms; observed [ThredUp terms][thredup-awin], [Etsy date][etsy-policy])*

For our search journey, an advertiser would plausibly distinguish: **a category/item query that exposes its inventory to someone who had not picked a site; an alert finding newly available stock; and a pasted exact marketplace listing from an already-decided shopper.** The first two can shift demand to that advertiser; the third has weaker acquisition value. Searching before clicking is evidence of intent, not evidence of incremental demand. With one-off stock, another buyer might have bought the same item anyway: measure total marketplace contribution or substitution as well as the referred person's order. *(inferred)*

What to show to earn a better rate: advertiser-approved randomized audience or geographically controlled exposure/holdout, measuring **total settled orders and contribution**, not just orders carrying our tag; merchant-verified new/lapsed buyers; net eligible order value, return/cancellation rates and subsequent buyer value; deliberate-click provenance and successful app handoff. Do not remove only the affiliate tag and call that an incrementality test: the helpful discovery exposure would still exist in both groups. A larger basket raises dollar commissions but only raises a rational percentage if margins or lifetime value improve. *(inferred; agent research proposal)*

### 4. What the marketplace can afford

Let `t = marketplace revenue / merchandise`, `q = incremental fraction`, `c = extra payments, fulfilment, support, fraud and other variable costs / merchandise`. A one-order, no-repeat-value break-even acquisition ceiling is approximately **`q × (t − c)`**, before network costs and desired profit. The requested **`t × q`** is an intentionally generous **gross revenue ceiling**, not a recommendation to spend that much. Repeats, supply growth and competitive subsidies can support larger initial payouts; finite stock/substitution can reduce them. *(inferred)*

They do not claim any marketplace or partner has those lift rates. “Fee proxy” means an example transaction tariff, not consolidated take rate; fees paid onward to a processor do not all become spendable profit. Unless specified, fee examples use **$33 merchandise, no shipping, tax or paid boost**. *(assumption; inferred)*
