# How other fields already solved our hard calls

Researched 2026-11-15 by Ben (Claude Code, thread "Resolve the product's ambiguous problems"). Feeds [spec/hard-calls.md](../../../spec/hard-calls.md). Method: RDD with IAE. For each messy problem, name its general structure, assume a field already solved that structure, find the mechanism, and give it a job here. The approved insurance, quant and appraisal mechanisms in the pitch thread's `market.md` (project files, `secondhand-pitch/market.md`) are not repeated; this note covers the problems they did not.

Ben, 06:59: "find a bunch of ambigious problems that need solving with logic and how the product is made ... use rdd research or iae and find adjacent fields" ([words](../../../product/words.md#2026-11-15-a-pitch-constitution-ambiguous-problems-and-the-judges-own-logic)).

**Marks.** *Observed*: the page was opened this session and says it (quotes are verbatim). *Asserted*: from memory or a search title, not reopened. *Inferred*: our reasoning.

## References, each with a job

| # | Our problem's structure | Field | Mechanism | Quote or fact | Mark | Job in the spec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | A cancel for "sold it elsewhere" costs the account | Marketplace rules | eBay counts it as a transaction defect, capped at 2% | A defect is when "The seller cancels the order unexpectedly (e.g. because it was out of stock, or because they sold it to someone else)"; the cap is "No more than 2% of transactions" ([eBay seller standards](https://www.ebay.com/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-standards-policy?id=4347)) | *observed* | Calls 1 and 9: the account's standing is a number with a ceiling |
| R2 | A lost dispute costs more for a small seller than a big one | Marketplace rules | eBay's case floor is a count, not only a rate | "You're allowed 2 cases closed without seller resolution within an evaluation period, or 0.3% of your transactions – whichever is higher" (same page) | *observed* | Call 9: for someone with 40 sales a year, 0.3% is 0.12 of a case, so the count of 2 binds and a third lost case is over the line (*inferred* arithmetic) |
| R3 | A second buyer while the first has not paid | Real estate | The backup offer: a signed second offer that takes over only if the first deal falls through; the seller does not break the first contract for it | Common practice (Bankrate's page did not load; Redfin, Rocket and HAR guides are in the search results) | *asserted* | Call 1 |
| R4 | Which of two offers is worth more | Trading and lending | Counterparty risk: an offer is worth its amount times the chance it settles | Standard expected-value practice | *asserted* | Call 2 |
| R5 | Several sources disagree about one fact | Master data management (Informatica, now part of Salesforce) | Survivorship: per field, rank sources by trust and pick the surviving value; trust can be downgraded by data quality | "The survivorship configuration uses the current downgrade trust score that you define for data quality"; "the most recently updated relationship attribute value survives" ([Informatica survivorship rules](https://onlinehelp.informatica.com/IICS/prod/b360/en/gg-b360-configure-survivorship/Rules_and_guidelines_for_survivorship.html)). Salesforce teaches the same idea as match, merge and survivorship ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/match-merge-and-survivorship/resolve-data-conflicts-with-merge-and-unmerge)) | *observed* (Informatica), *asserted* (Trailhead title) | Call 7 |
| R6 | A step failed after other steps succeeded | Distributed systems | Saga with compensating steps; each step idempotent; compensation is not a rewind | "A compensating transaction doesn't necessarily return the system data to its state at the start of the original operation"; "A step might run multiple times when retried, so design each step as an idempotent command" ([Azure, compensating transaction](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/architecture/patterns/compensating-transaction)) | *observed* | Call 6 |
| R7 | Keep many copies matching one record | Infrastructure | A control loop moves the current state toward the desired state, again and again | "The thermostat acts to bring the current state closer to the desired state" ([Kubernetes, controllers](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/architecture/controller/), page description; the body did not load) | *observed* (description only) | Call 6 |
| R8 | A site keeps failing; stop before it hurts accounts | Distributed systems | Circuit breaker: open fails at once; half-open lets a few through to test; one failure reopens | Open: "The request from the application fails immediately"; half-open: "A limited number of requests from the application are allowed to pass through"; "If any request fails, the circuit breaker assumes that the fault is still present, so it reverts to the Open state" ([Azure, circuit breaker](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/architecture/patterns/circuit-breaker)) | *observed* | Call 5 |
| R9 | When is a change big enough to act on | Accounting (SEC SAB 99) | Materiality: a size rule of thumb, overridden by qualitative factors | "...is not material in the absence of particularly egregious circumstances" (the 5% rule of thumb); "Qualitative factors may cause misstatements of quantitatively small amounts to be material" ([SAB 99](https://sec.gov/interps/account/sab99.htm)) | *observed* | Call 4: a fee change moves a live price only past a size, except when it crosses the person's floor |
| R10 | Is our model drifting in a category | Actuarial science | Actual-to-expected (A/E) ratio from experience studies; read above or below 1, and only when the interval excludes 1 | "Actual-to-Expected (A/E) analysis is the cornerstone of actuarial experience studies"; "A/E ratios above 100% indicate adverse ... experience" ([MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/actual-to-expected-analysis/)) | *observed* | Call 13 |
| R11 | Two ratings disagree; which to use | Bank capital rules (Basel II) | When two agencies' ratings differ, the higher risk weight (the worse rating) applies | Basel II, para 97 | *asserted* (memory) | Call 8: between two condition grades, list the lower |
| R12 | The person's silence | Contract law | Silence is not acceptance, except where the parties agreed it would be | Restatement (Second) of Contracts §69 | *asserted* (memory) | Call 3 |
| R13 | Waiting on a person who may not answer | Incident response | Escalation policies raise the level of a page as time passes, then hand off | PagerDuty-style escalation | *asserted* | Call 3 |
| R14 | An owner prices above the market | Behavioral economics | The endowment effect: owners ask about twice what buyers will pay for the same mug | Kahneman, Knetsch and Thaler, 1990 | *asserted* (memory) | Call 10: don't argue, let the market answer on a set date |
| R15 | Should we ask one more question | Decision analysis | Value of information: ask only when the answer could change the decision by more than the asking costs | Howard, 1966 | *asserted* (memory) | Call 11 |
| R16 | Valuing on an unproven assumption | Appraisal (USPAP) | An "extraordinary assumption" must be stated, and the value is only as good as it | USPAP definitions | *asserted* (memory) | Call 11: the upside is shown as conditional, never as the price |
| R17 | Acting on an old price | Trading | Never trade on a stale quote; mark positions to the market, not to the last trade | Standard practice | *asserted* | Call 12 |
| R18 | How much a small approval should authorize | Insurance underwriting and lending | Delegated authority: each person or system may approve up to a written limit; above it goes up a level | Standard practice ("letters of authority") | *asserted* | Call 13 |
| R19 | Settle or contest a small claim | Insurance claims | Small claims are fast-tracked and paid when investigating costs more than the claim | Standard practice (loss adjustment expense) | *asserted* | Call 9 |
