## What we are trying to beat, in one table

| Rival | What a person gets | How it gets it | Where we can be more accurate or more honest | Where it can beat us |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Terapeak | eBay average and range of sold prices, sell-through, accepted Best Offer amounts, 3 years | eBay's own transactions, exact keyword match ([teardown 1.8](../../../research/data/nate/teardown-price-tools-and-registries.md#18-ebay-product-research-terapeak)) | its range is the spread of whatever the keyword matched, no forward statement, no coverage, one site, variants mixed in | volume and true accepted prices on eBay; and eBay rows are `may_model: false` for us ([valuation §2](../../engines/valuation.md#2-signals)), so on eBay-heavy kinds our fitted parts learn from less data than a Terapeak user sees |
| 130point | accepted Best Offer prices across card marketplaces | not stated ([teardown 1.2](../../../research/data/nate/teardown-price-tools-and-registries.md#12-130point)) | cross-kind, a range with coverage | exact truth where we use a learned discount `d_offer,cat` |
| PriceCharting, Card Ladder, WatchCharts | a per-condition or per-grade price and index for one kind | curated identity (grade, reference), outlier review, asks when thin | a coverage statement, and cross-site | identity is a key in their kind; our q50 will struggle to beat their median there |
| Phia, Croissant | a label or one value | not published | sources and a checked range | nothing measured either way: the benchmark is the only evidence |
| FlipTip, Underpriced AI | a point and a range with vendor-run accuracy | photo to price, vendor tests ([flaws note](../../../research/price/nate/published-price-tests-and-their-flaws.md)) | a test without leakage, by price band, with unsold items | a photo-only path where we have no comps |
| ThriftX, Dupe, Gem, crosslisters, Keepa, Lyst, Shopify | no used-price range at all | n/a | row 7 does not apply; they compete on rows 1 to 6, 8 to 10 | n/a |

The honest edge is not "a better median". It is a range whose stated share holds, a "not sure" that is earned, and every number open to its sales. The improvements below are ranked by how much they protect that edge.

## What is already right and should not be touched

- **The backtest plan's split and freeze rules**: rolling origin by month with a 7-day gap, one locked month scored once, each estimate frozen the day before listing and a locked item frozen at the lock date ([plan decisions 1 to 4](../backtest/plan.md#how-the-data-is-split)). This is what both vendor tests lack.
- **The leakage checks that refuse a run** ([backtest.py `check_leakage`](../../../jobs/valuation/backtest.py)): future comps, the target, `may_model`, at-ask, priced-from-our-number, relists by cluster; and the self-test with an oracle, random comps and a planted leak ([experiment.py `self_test`](../../../jobs/valuation/experiment.py)).
- **By-product bootstrap intervals** ([experiment.py `_lift`](../../../jobs/valuation/experiment.py)), cells under 30 printed as "too few", and the price band taken from our own q50, not the truth ([backtest.py `run_backtest`](../../../jobs/valuation/backtest.py)).
- **PPE20 as the headline, beside the measured noise ceiling and the value of abstaining** ([plan decisions 8, 10, 13](../backtest/plan.md#what-is-measured)). The ceiling turns "70%" into a claim a judge can weigh.
- **The forecast duel** as the only fair way to compare a rival, with its hash as pre-registration ([plan §7](../backtest/plan.md#the-forecast-duel-the-cheapest-clean-test-and-it-compares-competitors)).
- **Pre-written result sentences keyed on the interval, never the point** ([experiment.py `sentence`](../../../jobs/valuation/experiment.py)), and the proposal that a cell fails only when its Wilson interval lies wholly outside the band ([plan §6](../backtest/plan.md#6-fresh-review-2026-11-15)). Take that proposal; #5 builds on it.
- **Asks never set the value; retail is never a comp; unsold rows are censored, not dropped** ([money D1](../../determinations/money.md#d1-comparable-set-and-weights), [D10](../../determinations/money.md#d10-chance-of-sale-within-a-period-at-a-price)).
- **Log-scale MAD trim, Kish `n_eff`, gross-adjustment weighting and the bracketing flag** ([comps.ts](../../../app/src/engines/valuation/comps.ts)). Standard appraisal practice, written out and testable.
- **The citation with table versions, param versions and a basis hash** ([proof.ts](../../../app/src/engines/proof/proof.ts), [product `citation`](../../product.md)). No rival in parity can recompute a shown number; this can.
- **Person-scoped params never read another person's row** ([param.ts `lineage`](../../../app/src/engines/param/param.ts)).
- **The D12 double-sale probability from detection windows and time to sale** ([money D12 step 3](../../determinations/money.md#d12-best-site-or-set-of-sites)), replacing a fixed 1%.

## Open questions only Nate or Ben can answer

1. **eBay rows and `may_model: false`.** If it stands, eBay-heavy kinds cannot be fitted on eBay sales, and our accuracy there will lag a Terapeak user's view. Do we claim those kinds at all on the website, or show them as "priced from other sites"?
2. **Random price jitter (#10).** A logged ±3% on the suggested ask, with consent, is the cheapest way to measure how price moves speed. Is any randomisation of a person's price acceptable to you?
3. **The 80% target.** Is "8 in 10" the promise, or would you rather show a 50% range (narrower, easier to read) as the main one and keep 80% behind a tap? This changes D5, D6 and the benchmark's headline.
4. **Benchmark row 7 at hackathon scale.** At 20 to 60 resolved sales by the demo, a lift reads as a direction only. Do we show row 7 on the website at that size, or hold it until 100 resolve?
5. **A losing public number.** The Kaggle Mercari case may land well short of the 0.3875 winner. Publish it anyway, as the benchmark's rule 5 implies?
6. **Paid rival plans.** Terapeak needs a seller login and the guides (#15) have paid tiers; buying one changes what row 7 can compare against ([benchmark open list](../../benchmark.md#open-for-nate-and-ben)).
