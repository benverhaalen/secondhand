# How he gives input without typing, and how a term is taught in passing

Open when: asking him anything about a UI, or when he drops an image, video or site. His words: "what i really dont want is having to waste my brain power and attention on typing out essays"; "selecting areas or combining words with images or video or sites".

## Input

1. He drops an image, a video or a site, then taps its job: layout, feel, density, color and type, motion, avoid.
2. Picks between images always carry "neither" and "close, but" buttons. Picking may anchor him on what was shown (eliciting-from-his-head.md:19) and a click can read as agreement, so a pick is weaker evidence than a typed reason he volunteers.
3. Pointing at an area: a screenshot with a dragged box. Clicking an element in the running app needs an element picker; every one in the notes is an MCP server, so that waits for his yes.
4. A vague word gets a few translations as buttons ("cleaner": less on screen, more space, quieter color).
5. Video: `python3 -I scripts/video_frames.py <video> --every <seconds>` or `--scene <threshold>`, with `--sheet 4x3` for one contact image; frame names carry timestamps for motion timing. Sites are opened only with the Playwright scripts here.
6. Questions are buttons with the consequence of each option. Nothing he must open and read; show what he needs in chat.

## A term taught in passing

A design term appears once in the label of the option it names, for example "tighter spacing (called density)", and the same word is used after that. Motion terms come from `.agents/vendor/emil/animation-vocabulary/SKILL.md`, quoted as its glossary words them. A link to a source he can trust is a separate button. Nothing is explained unless he asks. His words: "it needs to also somewhat educate me ... but i dont want it to become a teaching tool".
