# How evidence is judged and shown

The rules every other constitution follows when it gives a reason, uses a number or makes a claim. Asked for by Ben at [08:59](../words.md#2026-11-15-every-why-explained-numbers-that-help-and-a-statistic-we-can-defend): "ensure in whatever constitution all the evidence and decisions need to be explained as to why. just saying 70 20 isnt a why. also those numbers arent even helpful." *Agent proposal*, checked against Ben's resume, project descriptions and project evidence guidance. General patterns from that reading are recorded below; private career details stay outside this repo.

## The problem

A rule that says "within 20%" or "70% coverage" tells you a line was drawn, not why it is there or what a person loses if it's crossed. A judge, a teammate or a later agent can't tell whether the line still matters, and a seller can't tell what it means for them. A quote from Ben records who decided, but it doesn't explain the decision. So every reason has to name the cause and what it does to the person, and every number has to earn its place by changing a decision or by being something the person feels.

**Success, checkably:** for any rule in any constitution, a reader can answer "what goes wrong for whom if we break this?" from the rule alone, and every number in it is in units a seller feels or names the decision it moves.

## Rules

**E1. A why is a mechanism and a consequence.** Name what causes what, and what that costs or gives the person. Put the source after the cause. *Why:* without the cause, a teammate cannot apply a rule to a new case or retire it when it stops helping. The person then pays for a rule kept past its purpose.

**E2. A number stays only if it changes a decision or a person feels it.** Use dollars, days, seconds, taps or useful scope. For a percentage, name the quantity and baseline; keep absolute counts available. Explain what a test threshold decides and why that line was chosen. *Why:* a large percentage can hide a tiny change or denominator. A seller or judge needs to know what actually improves before deciding whether the result matters ([08:59](../words.md#2026-11-15-every-why-explained-numbers-that-help-and-a-statistic-we-can-defend): "those numbers arent even helpful").

**E3. Lead with what the person can now do, then the mechanism and proof.** State the useful whole and the distinction that makes it worth using. Use the person's units when measuring impact. A demonstrated capability can stand without a number. *Why:* an unfamiliar reader needs to understand the product before judging a component score. Leading with the easiest metric can hide the very capability that saves the seller work.

**E4. Comparative claims need a relevant baseline under comparable conditions.** Compare with what people would really do today, on the same items and starting states where possible. Label uncontrolled historical comparisons as descriptive. For a capability claim, show the action succeeding against stated acceptance criteria; a baseline is needed only if claiming an improvement. *Why:* different items or conditions can produce the apparent gain. A matched comparison lets the judge attribute the difference to the method, while a direct demonstration lets a buyer assess a useful new capability without an invented percentage.

**E5. Defend it before you claim it.** Retain the measurement method, raw quantities, main assumption, failure cases and a way to reproduce the result. Check what would make the claim false and whether an easy shortcut could earn the same score. Efficiency claims retain quality, retries and failures. *Why:* a score that rewards a shortcut or omits failed attempts promises savings the seller may never receive. A judge needs evidence that the evaluator detects the failure the claim says we prevent ([08:59](../words.md#2026-11-15-every-why-explained-numbers-that-help-and-a-statistic-we-can-defend): "creating a statistic and being able to defend it").

**E6. Say where it holds and where it doesn't.** Keep the items, sites, conditions and contribution boundaries that change the claim's meaning. Distinguish researched, built, tested in isolation, integrated and demonstrated in use. Simulation, adoption and code inspection each support different claims. *Why:* a component test does not show the complete journey works, and use does not show improved outcomes. Blurring those boundaries leads the buyer to rely on a capability we have not shown ([revenue R6](revenue.md)).

**E7. Flattering is allowed; hiding isn't.** Select the strongest relevant supported capability or result, including a useful distinction without numbers. State selected conditions, keep losses and exclusions, and publish the result whichever way it goes. *Why:* the most impressive percentage may say less than a capability the reader needs. Hiding a loss makes the remaining evidence misleading and costs the judge trust in the whole product ([07:18](../words.md#2026-11-15-a-backtest-that-flatters-truthfully-seasons-and-waves-and-merge-to-main)).

**E8. Every figure carries its mark and a path to its source.** Use observed, asserted or inferred from [features-proof.md](../features-proof.md#how-to-read-the-proof-marks). Show the useful claim and essential limit first; keep detailed methods reachable behind it. *Why:* the mark tells the reader how much reliance the evidence warrants. Layering the detail lets a stranger understand the value quickly and a judge inspect the support without making either read an audit report.

## Where these come from

Observed in the resume and project descriptions: the lead establishes the useful product. Later lines explain distinctive mechanisms and add selected proof. Quantities describe actual resources, useful scale, validation scope or demonstrated use. Useful capabilities also appear without numbers. Generic task lists and exhaustive methods do not carry the presentation.

The resume guidance and project-evidence skill make the selection method explicit: recover the full capability before choosing a metric, compare the strongest relevant facts, and preserve the distinction when simplifying. Baselines support improvement claims; acceptance criteria support capability claims. Failures, quality tradeoffs and implementation status stay with the evidence. Detailed methods remain available without crowding the headline.
