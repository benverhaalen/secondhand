# The website constitution

What the public site must do, for whom, and how signed-in web relates to the app. How any screen is decided is the [UI constitution](../ui-constitution.md); how it looks is the [brand constitution](../../brand/constitution.md); the page map is [spec/pages.md §6.17](../../spec/pages.md#617-the-website); the hero work so far is in [brand/explore/web-home/](../../brand/explore/web-home/). Reasons and numbers follow [evidence.md](evidence.md). *Agent proposal*; the public-site rules retire after judging, the signed-in rules stay.

## The problem

Two people read the site, and neither is using the product yet. A **judge** after the table wants to know whether what they saw is real and how deep it goes. A **stranger** has never heard of secondhand, is wary of an app that signs in to their marketplace accounts and changes their photos, and gives the page a few seconds. The site has to make the idea knowable from one line, answer the worries that a new and odd product raises (accounts, honest photos, money, safety), prove itself with working demos and real numbers, and show where it goes at scale. It has to be fast, alive, and plainly the same brand as the app without looking like the app ([04:07](../words.md#2026-11-15-one-place-makes-the-ui-every-correction-becomes-a-check-emils-skills-vet-every-page-and-the-system-designs-any-new-page): "the website cant look like the app"). Signed in, the site is the app laid out for a mouse and a big screen, not a second product.

**Success, checkably:** a stranger shown the hero for five seconds can say what it does; every worry in [faq.md](../faq.md) has a page or answer reachable in two clicks; every number on the site carries its proof mark; the page passes break-ui at phone, tablet and desktop sizes.

## Ends

| End | Test | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| The idea lands in one line | The five-second test above | A stranger can decide whether to keep reading. |
| Every worry has an answer | FAQ and the small pages exist, written from [qa.md](../qa.md), not invented | Unresolved account or money concerns can stop someone trying the product. |
| It proves, not claims | Each feature section shows a demo of the real app or a measured number | A visitor can inspect behavior or evidence before trusting the promise. |
| It feels alive and ours | Motion on first load and first scroll; no section reads as a template | Purposeful motion directs attention without hiding the explanation. |

## Rules

**W1. One line, then "See how it works".** The line alone carries the hero; no period; the second "secondhand" in the wordmark face; hands sized to the wordmark. *Why:* A single line lets a stranger identify the useful capability before spending attention on details. The next action lets them inspect it. The typography, the missing period and the hand sizing keep the hero built from the same wordmark, type and hands as the app icon, so a stranger who later opens the app sees the same mark they met on the site (*inferred*; Ben kept the rule at [09:23](../words.md#2026-11-15-five-reasons-answered-cleos-style-in-the-apps-family-and-site-images-through-codex)). **W2. Alive, and every motion has a reason.** The open is the hands stepping in beside the wordmark, moved in code from the icon, with no dap and no generated frames ([09:21](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-first-screen-our-fonts-one-family-with-the-app-no-gradient-a-shadow-under-the-text-a-gradient-hover-hands-moved-in-code), ledger L58); behind the line, a slow blurred wall of many real things, never repeating visibly; the first scroll has the strongest animation. Motion never blocks reading or loading. *Why:* Motion can direct attention to the wordmark and the next section. If it delays loading or blocks reading, the stranger waits before learning what the product does. Source: "on the website i want it to be moving. feel alive... bold moves need purpose too"; "great web design... optimal loading and super fast stuff is important". *Why:* Motion can direct attention to the wordmark and the next section. If it delays loading or blocks reading, the stranger waits before learning what the product does. Source: ([01:11](../words.md#2026-11-15-a-brand-system-for-anything-alive-and-bold-with-purpose-code-that-knows-each-app-and-knowing-when-things-sell)); ([04:32](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-website-references-story-photography-signed-in-web-and-the-buysell-toggle)).

**W3. Show with the real app.** Feature sections use recordings of the built app driven on demo data, the way the Magic UW demo video was made. No drawn screen stands in for one that exists. *Why:* A recording exposes the actual interaction and its limits. A drawn screen could promise an action the stranger cannot perform in the product. Source: ([05:32](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-visitors-path-the-first-scroll-demos-driven-on-mock-data-impressive-versus-housekeeping-show-versus-tell)).

**W4. Impressive gets space, housekeeping folds.** The sites we connect to and what we do on them get a section; connecting them does not. *Why:* Space spent on setup pushes the useful outcome farther down the page. Giving the connected sites and their actions space helps a stranger decide whether the product does work they need. Source: ([05:32](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-visitors-path-the-first-scroll-demos-driven-on-mock-data-impressive-versus-housekeeping-show-versus-tell)).

**W5. The small pages exist.** How it works, sites and fees, the data and Databricks, FAQ, privacy, pricing, status, about and what's next. *Why:* Account access, money and photo changes raise different questions. Separate reachable answers let a stranger resolve the concern that would otherwise stop them trying the app. Source: "have those little pages most vibecoded sites disregard"; "with such a new and oddly constructed and worrysome product... a lot of concerns across the board". *Why:* Account access, money and photo changes raise different questions. Separate reachable answers let a stranger resolve the concern that would otherwise stop them trying the app. Source: ([05:05](../words.md#2026-11-15-edge-cases-from-a-mentor-site-audiences-two-kinds-of-photo-enhancement-and-measuring-it-all)); ([05:28](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-public-site-one-section-at-a-time-its-purposes-the-worries-and-the-future)).

**W6. Honest numbers and honest photos.** Every figure carries its mark from [features-proof.md](../features-proof.md#how-to-read-the-proof-marks). About photos the site says "every AI photo is measured against your tape before it posts", never that the AI is accurate. The prior report describes a raw tee redraw about 2 inches too narrow before correction. *Why:* An AI photo can change an item's shape, and a figure can imply stronger proof than exists. Showing the measurement check and evidence mark lets the seller or stranger judge what is actually supported. Source: (*asserted* here from [the photo proof](../features-proof.md#selling), not remeasured in this review).

**W7. Story general enough to see yourself in.** No single persona carries the site; the future and scale get their own section: monitoring and managing markets for you, an intelligence engine, pricing anything you own. *Why:* A single persona can make another visitor assume the product is not for them. A broader story shows the shared job, while a separate future section lets the judge distinguish ambition from current capability. Source: ([05:32](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-visitors-path-the-first-scroll-demos-driven-on-mock-data-impressive-versus-housekeeping-show-versus-tell), [06:01](../words.md#2026-11-15-show-the-magic-clips-basement-demos-faster-loops-and-the-intelligence-engine)).

**W8. Cleo's style, adapted to how the app looks.** Take Cleo's style rather than its layout, and adapt it to the app's UI, so the site and the app read as one family. Shorter than Cleo's page, and dark by default. *Why:* a stranger who meets the site and then the app should see one brand, not two products. A site that looks borrowed from someone else breaks that trust right when they decide whether to sign in. Source: ([09:23](../words.md#2026-11-15-five-reasons-answered-cleos-style-in-the-apps-family-and-site-images-through-codex); earlier [05:05](../words.md#2026-11-15-edge-cases-from-a-mentor-site-audiences-two-kinds-of-photo-enhancement-and-measuring-it-all), [05:49](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-hero-the-line-alone-ambient-clothing-motion-dark-by-default-the-hands-and-the-wordmark-face-on-words)).

**W9. Built one section at a time with Ben, then faster.** Each ambiguous section gets a board of options like the dark-mode previews; once the primitives are set, the rest goes quickly. *Why:* An ambiguous section can carry the wrong story through the whole site. Comparing options with Ben before repeating its primitives lets the stranger receive one coherent explanation. Source: ([05:28](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-public-site-one-section-at-a-time-its-purposes-the-worries-and-the-future)).

**W10. Every screen size is broken on purpose.** Phone (with a menu), tablet and desktop pass break-ui; store logos are rounded like the app; say "App Store coming soon". *Why:* Clipped text or an unreachable menu can hide the only route to an answer. Testing each screen size lets a stranger reach the same information on their device. Coming-soon wording avoids promising an unavailable download. The store download badges are rounded like the app icon, so the one place where the site hands off to the app looks like the app (*inferred*; Ben kept the rule at [09:23](../words.md#2026-11-15-five-reasons-answered-cleos-style-in-the-apps-family-and-site-images-through-codex)). This covers the store badges only. Marketplace logos keep their own corners ([brand R26](../../brand/constitution.md)). Source: ([07:09](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-hero-on-every-screen-one-baseline-for-both-faces-no-period-a-menu-on-phones-rounded-store-logos-the-hand-options)); ([05:41](../words.md#2026-11-15-a-demo-that-stuns-science-fair-judging-and-claims-that-change-how-judges-see-it)).

**W11. Rules are scoped to a surface.** A rule written for the app binds the web only if the web has the same problem; mouse and touch differ. *Why:* Touch and a mouse offer different ways to reach and act on information. Copying a rule without its cause can leave a web visitor unable to find or use a control. Source: ([03:42](../words.md#2026-11-15-rules-per-surface-and-research-per-surface-in-the-loop)); ([pointer-and-touch.md](../../research/cross-cutting/ben/website/pointer-and-touch.md)).

**W12. Signed-in web loses nothing the app has, except the camera.** Photos are uploaded instead of shot; the camera hands off to the phone. The web adds what a big screen is good at: bulk actions, folder drops, label printing. *Why:* People moving to a larger screen still need their existing tasks. Preserving them avoids forcing a seller back to the phone, while bulk actions reduce repeated work across items. Source: ([20:45](../words.md#2026-11-14-app-first-and-ui-factory-after-the-brand)).

**W13. Images.** Real photos for items. Generated images only for things that aren't the product, touched up so they don't read as AI, made through the Codex CLI with the [image-gen skill](../../.agents/skills/image-gen/SKILL.md). *Why:* Item photos are evidence of what is for sale. Generated substitutes could give a stranger a false expectation of the product; keeping them to other imagery preserves that distinction. The route is Ben's choice ([09:23](../words.md#2026-11-15-five-reasons-answered-cleos-style-in-the-apps-family-and-site-images-through-codex)). It is the same one the repo already uses for every generated image, so one loop and one way of judging images covers the site too. Source: ([22:23](../words.md#2026-11-14-the-ui-loop)); ([04:32](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-website-references-story-photography-signed-in-web-and-the-buysell-toggle)).

**W14. One family with the app, held by checks.** The site builds from the same sources as the app, and each of Ben's five causes of drift has a check that fails a site page or a new board ([09:24](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-first-screen-our-fonts-one-family-with-the-app-no-gradient-a-shadow-under-the-text-a-gradient-hover-hands-moved-in-code)):

| Cause of drift | What holds it | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A second source of truth | The UI constitution, `words.md` and the app's parts decide; Cleo is a tone reference only (W8) | This file; the critic's fail list for every image call (W15) |
| Values made up in place | Colors come from `brand/tokens.css` (S1); spacing, radius and weight sit on the brand scales | `.agents/checks/site.mjs`, `.agents/checks/brand.mjs` |
| The app's rules not run | Every B and U rule runs on every render at phone and desktop width, and a brand error fails the run | `hero/check.mjs`, `boards/render.mjs`, `web-boards.yml` |
| A fallback face | A render whose brand faces don't load fails | the same three, and B15 in `scripts/brand-check.mjs` |
| Site-only ideas | A device the app doesn't use (a text shadow, a filter, a mask, a gradient, a keyframe animation, glass) cites the Locked ledger row where Ben said yes (S2). A Locked row can waive a rendered check only inside `data-ledger="<row>"` | `.agents/checks/site.mjs`, `.agents/checks/render.mjs` |

*Why:* a stranger who meets the site and then the app should see one product, and a rule a script holds doesn't drift between agents. The first screen, `site.css` and every new board are held; the older pages and the picked boards (01 to 03) get warnings until their turn. Source: ([09:24](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-first-screen-our-fonts-one-family-with-the-app-no-gradient-a-shadow-under-the-text-a-gradient-hover-hands-moved-in-code)).

**W15. Site primitives grow from app parts, and each is decided the same way.** Take Cleo's rhythm, not its look: one idea per screen, large confident type, short lines, generous space, one accent per section, restrained motion. Leave its colors, illustration style, mascot energy and grid. Each primitive scales up an app part and is built from app tokens; anything not listed needs Ben's yes first ([09:26](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-first-screen-our-fonts-one-family-with-the-app-no-gradient-a-shadow-under-the-text-a-gradient-hover-hands-moved-in-code)).

| Primitive | Grows from (app part) | Why that parent |
|---|---|---|
| Nav pill | The tab pill | The pages are places, as the app's tabs are |
| Hero | The wordmark with the hands, one line, one action | The app's top: the mark, then the one thing to do |
| Section band | The state-gradient lead card, page wide; the page's only gradient, one per page at most | The app spends its gradient on the one thing that matters most |
| Proof block | The "Why?" card: the claim first, the number on tap or hover, its source | The app already shows where a number comes from |
| Before and after | The photo toggle, as a draggable reveal | The same comparison the seller makes in the app |
| Phone frame | Real app screens, never redrawn (W3) | A drawn screen can promise what the app doesn't do |
| Store badges | Rounded like the app icon (W10) | The hand-off to the app looks like the app |
| Q and A | The folding shelves | Folding is how the app keeps housekeeping out of the way (W4) |
| Footer | The same type scale, nothing new | Nothing at the end needs a new part |

Every primitive goes through one loop: (1) the job, the audience (a judge with three minutes, a seller, a buyer) and the success signal; (2) the constraints: the brand rules, its app parent, the UI constitution, contrast and target minimums, the evidence rules; (3) two or three expert options, with GPT Image through Codex for composition only (W13); (4) a senior reviewer's critique of each: clear in five seconds, fits the family, claims honest, accessible, cost to build, and its main risk; (5) one recommendation with its reason and what would change it, and Ben picks; (6) the pick goes into this file and the ledger, and into a check where a script can hold it; (7) the checks pass, and someone who hasn't seen it says what it does after five seconds. Ben runs the loop in chat, one part at a time: each round shows the part so far as an image in the brand faces and two to four options for the next part, each with how it looks, its words and an expert's why. He says when to go on; his feedback is applied, and any part of it a script can hold becomes a check before the next part ([10:11](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-website-one-part-at-a-time-with-bens-feedback-turned-into-checks)). *Why:* the same reasoning on every part is what makes the parts look related. Source: ([09:26](../words.md#2026-11-15-the-first-screen-our-fonts-one-family-with-the-app-no-gradient-a-shadow-under-the-text-a-gradient-hover-hands-moved-in-code)).
