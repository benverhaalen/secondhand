# Who the designs lean toward

Eight people, each defined by what they want from one visit, not by age or job. The image loop leans each first-round design toward one of them ([frontend-standard §2](frontend-standard.md#personas-for-the-first-round)). The set is an *agent proposal*. It comes from [the first person we build for](decisions.md#2026-11-14-the-first-person-we-build-for-is-someone-clearing-their-own-things), from [what sellers and buyers want](../research/cross-cutting/ben/user-wants.md), and from the three selling intents Ben named ([words](words.md#2026-11-15-the-new-button-gradients-that-carry-state-seller-intents-onboarding-and-build-ready)).

| Person | Their situation | What they want | What they fear | The line a design call gets | What it rests on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Decided as the first person |

## Using them

- **Take two to four per screen**, the ones who use that screen. Capture and Sell lean toward the four sellers. Buy leans toward the hunter. Today takes whoever has something waiting.
- **The line gives the situation, never the layout.** It says who is looking and what they want, so the model works out the screen.
- **When two people's wants conflict**, the one clearing out wins, because they are the first person we build for. Better still, turn the conflict into something the person sets once, such as their minimum or "sell it fast".
- **A want is only as good as what it rests on.** The last column says whether a person's want was seen in what people did, or only named. A design that wins for an unsourced person has not won yet: the judge says so, and the demo is where it gets tested.
- **Add a person only when a real one shows up** in the demo or in research who wants something none of these do.
